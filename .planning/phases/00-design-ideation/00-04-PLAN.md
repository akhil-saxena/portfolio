<objective>
Build the charcoal theme as a running prototype inside the playground — tokens in one file,
font faces in another, per D-29's split — and stand up the three static checks that make
DSGN-05 a tested specification rather than an assertion.

Purpose: RESEARCH measured that the cascade hazard is not a specificity bug (D-27's
arithmetic is correct) but an exhaustiveness bug: a charcoal token declared in the light
block and not restated in the dark block ties `:root.dark` at (0,2,0), and the winner is
then decided by whichever stylesheet Astro emitted last. The exhaustiveness invariant is the
root-cause fix, and it is the single most load-bearing rule in this phase. The contrast and
font-name checks close the two other DSGN-05 failure modes measured during research.

Output: `theme-charcoal.css`, `fonts-charcoal.css`, three green measurement scripts, and
findings evidence for AAA-1 and G-11.
</objective>

<interfaces>
Block-structure contract, verified from `../design-system/src/tokens.css` — the formatting
is load-bearing because both `block()` implementations find the block end with
`css.indexOf("\n}", open)`:

- Selectors: `:root[data-brand="charcoal"] {` for light at (0,2,0), and
  `:root[data-brand="charcoal"].dark {` for dark at (0,3,0)
- Blocks must be FLAT — no nested rules, no nested at-rules — and the closing brace must sit
  at column 0. An indented brace or a nested rule silently truncates the parsed token set and
  the exhaustiveness check then passes for the wrong reason.
- `tokens.css` declares its dark block as the selector list `:root.dark, .dark { … }`; each
  selector carries its own specificity, so charcoal light at (0,2,0) beats bare `.dark`
  (0,1,0) unconditionally and ties only with `:root.dark`.
