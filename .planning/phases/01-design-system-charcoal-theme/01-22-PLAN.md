<objective>
Rebuild charcoal as a **near-monochrome** theme: true-neutral surfaces, no accent hue, and semantic
colour retained only where it carries meaning.

**This is a design decision made by Akhil during 01-20 Task 3, and the reason is measurable rather
than aesthetic.** `--ochre` sits at **hue 32.2°**. Sampling all 39 photographs, the 30–45° band is
**14.8%** of their chromatic pixels and 15–30° adds **9.4%** — so the interface accent lived inside
the second-largest colour band of the content it was supposed to frame. It could neither recede nor
read as distinct. And `--cream` is **hue 42° at 31% saturation** — a beige, not a white — so the
neutrals were tinted the same family as the accent. His words: *"cream and dull yellow… it should not
be so dull… the interface should not take away from the photographs."*

The brief he gave: **modern and clean, present and accessible, photographs carry the colour, no dull
yellow or beige.** He chose near-monochrome from four built candidates rendered on his own content,
and kept **Playfair Display** after comparing five display faces — so **the typography does not
change at all.** This plan touches colour only.

**Two open findings dissolve rather than needing fixes.** Every one of the 11 remaining charcoal a11y
violations involves `#b0722a`: **G2** is `--amber-l` flattening onto `--ochre` (4 violations, 17
nodes), **G3** is `--ochre` used as text on a pinned near-black surface (7 violations, 7 nodes). The
triage called G2 *"a theme decision, not an edit"*. This is that decision. Verify they are gone rather
than assuming it.

Output: a charcoal that is actually charcoal.
</objective>

<interfaces>
**The mechanism is unchanged — only the target moves.** `--ochre` and `--ochre-d` are consumed
**zero** times directly in `primitives.css`; charcoal works entirely by aliasing the `--amber*` family
onto them. `--amber` alone has **66** consumers. So this plan re-points those aliases at a neutral
ramp; it does not touch a single component.

**Semantic tokens stay chromatic.** `--red` (22 consumers) and `--green` (12) carry error and success.
Removing their hue would be an accessibility regression, not a style choice — colour must never be the
only channel, but it must not be *absent* either.

**The status badges are already monochrome.** Plan 01-18 moved D-45's three statuses onto the **ink
ramp**, because no hue-derived triad could clear contrast in all four brand × mode cells. That fix
lands exactly right here — do not undo it.

**Proposed ramp** (adjust if measurement demands, and say so):

```
dark   page #0d0d0f   surf #17171a   surf2 #1e1e22   text #f2f2f4   muted #8e8e97   wire #2a2a30
light  page #fafafb   surf #ffffff   surf2 #f4f4f6   text #111114   muted #5c5c66   wire #d8d8de
```

`--focus` becomes a **neutral** — near-white on dark, near-black on light — and `--focus-ring-soft`
keeps the `color-mix(in srgb, var(--focus) N%, transparent)` derivation plan 01-21's predecessor
introduced, so it follows automatically.
</interfaces>
