<interfaces>
The six canonical viewports, from the responsive contract §1. Take them from a single declared table
in the spec file; do not scatter literals through the assertions.

    1 folded cover          344 × 882    coarse
    2 phone portrait        390 × 844    coarse
    3 foldable unfolded     841 × 768    coarse   ← aspect 1.095, the single most demanding capture
    4 tablet portrait       768 × 1024   coarse
    5 tablet landscape     1024 × 768    ambiguous
    6 laptop               1440 × 900    fine

The six public routes to walk: `/`, `/work`, `/photos`, one `/photos/<category>`, one
`/photos/<category>/<slug>`, `/resume`. Pick the category and the photograph by deriving them from
the data (first by `order`, say) rather than by naming one, so the audit follows the content.

**This audit runs locally and NOT in CI**, and that is a decision with a reason: a generator is
deterministic per-machine, not per-platform — libvips encodes differently on darwin/arm64 than on
ubuntu/x64, and the same class of divergence applies to font rasterisation and sub-pixel layout. A
cross-platform pixel assertion would be a flake generator. It also avoids a browser download on
every push. Phase 8 owns the decision about whether any of this becomes a CI gate under QUAL-01 and
QUAL-04; record that hand-off explicitly.

**Two mutation controls, and the reason there are two.** "Exactly one viewport" is two requirements
wearing one declaration, and each fails in its own direction:

    a 60svh  mutation on state A must break **fills**
    a 160svh mutation must break **departs**

**Phase 0's plan specified only the second and it could not fail** — a shorter state A departs more
easily, not less. Both controls are mandatory and both must be recorded with the assertion they
broke.
</interfaces>
